Falling-block games, three ways

Three ideas for a falling-block game with two modes: Solo, and Versus, where good combos drop obstacles into your opponent's tank. Each one is drawn here in Lagoon with the Sea deck. The names are working titles.

Why not Tetris itself

Tetris is one particular game, and its owners guard its look closely: the tall well, the seven four-square pieces, the way rows clear. Pond Hop is our own game in the Aggravation family, and I'd do the same here: the same kind of fun, with our own pieces, rules and name. Mean Bean Machine and Yoshi are inspiration in the same way, not something to copy.

What all three have in common

  • Solo: play for as long as you can. It speeds up slowly, and your best score is kept. When the tank fills up, your character dozes off and a card shows your score with Play again. No harsh "Game over".
  • Versus: against your deck's racer, like Speed (the octopus here, or the fox, bee, moth or cat in other decks), with Gentle, Steady and Quick paces. The first tank to fill up loses, and a tally keeps count.
  • Obstacles show up before they land. A little row above your tank shows what's coming, and a good move of your own can cancel them first.
  • It pauses from the menu or when you leave the app, the same as Speed.
  • Two phones later: easier than Speed, because each person has their own tank. Only the obstacles travel between the phones.
  • The pieces have no faces, so the tank stays calm to look at. Every sea friend has its own shape (tentacles, wings, star points, a shell, trailing strands), so you can tell them apart without color. The sounds are soft pops that climb a little higher with each combo.
A

Coral Stack

Closest to Tetris

How it plays

  • Pieces of coral drift down into a tank 8 blocks wide. Every piece has three blocks, either straight or bent. They're smaller than Tetris pieces, so they're quicker to place on a phone.
  • Fill a row from side to side and a wave washes it away.
  • Our twist: after a wave, loose blocks sink like sand into the holes below them. That can fill another row, and the next wave is a chain.

On the phone

Left, Turn, Right and Drop buttons along the bottom. You can also drag the piece with your finger, tap to turn it and flick down to drop it. A faint outline shows where it will land.

Versus

Wash away two rows at once, or make a chain, and sandbars push up from the bottom of the octopus's tank. Each sandbar has one gap; fill it to wash the sandbar away.

GoodThe most familiar feel, and the sinking-sand chains give it a style of its own.
Watch outFilling rows is the idea closest to Tetris, and steering shapes is the fiddliest of the three on a small screen.
B

Bubble Pals

Inspired by Mean Bean Machine

How it plays

  • Pairs of sea friends drop into a tank 6 wide: octopus, manta, starfish, turtle and jellyfish.
  • Four or more of the same friend touching, side by side or stacked, pop. Whatever was resting on them drops down and can pop more. That's a chain.
  • Our twist: every 25 pops, a pearl drops in. Wherever it lands, it pops every friend of the kind it touches.

On the phone

The same buttons and gestures as Coral Stack, but you only steer a pair, so it's simpler.

Versus

Each chain sends sleepy clams into the octopus's tank, and longer chains send many more: two pops in a row send 3 clams, three send 6, four send 12. Clams don't match anything. They wake up and leave when friends pop next to them.

GoodCombos and attacks are the heart of this kind of game, so Versus is the most exciting. Easy to learn, with real skill in setting up chains.
Watch outChains take a few games to get the hang of. Solo needs the pearl, or something like it, to stay interesting.
C

Tide Swap

Inspired by Yoshi

How it plays

  • Sea friends drift down into four columns. You don't steer them.
  • Instead, you tap one of the three swap buttons to trade two neighboring columns, so each friend lands on its twin. When two of the same meet, they swim away.
  • Clam shells: a bottom half and a top half come down separately. Land the top on a column with a bottom in it, and everything in between is closed up into a pearl for a big bonus.

On the phone

Only taps, on three buttons. The easiest of the three to play with one hand.

Versus

Close a clam, or make two matches in a row, and pebbles drop onto the octopus's columns. Two pebbles that meet disappear like a match, so they get in the way without walling you in.

GoodThe calmest and most relaxing of the three. Great for a quick game.
Watch outThe least strategy, so Versus is lighter. The clam shells are the part borrowed most directly from Yoshi; I'd want a twist of our own there before building it.

My pick

Bubble Pals

You asked for a game where combos throw obstacles at your opponent. In Bubble Pals the combos are the whole game, not an extra. Of the two where you steer falling pieces, it has the simplest controls, and the sea friends fit Tidepool's art. Coral Stack is the one to pick if the rows-of-blocks feel matters most to you. Tide Swap would make a lovely calm game later, but it's the weakest for Versus.

To narrow it down

  1. Which one feels right? Mixing is fine too, for example Bubble Pals with Tide Swap's clam shells.
  2. Controls: buttons, finger gestures, or both?
  3. Solo: endless with a best score, or short levels that end?
  4. How fast should Versus get at its quickest pace?
